# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Multiple households: singles, couples, full families. A private, self-hosted circle. Each household is its own tenant; members share a login space and see each other's activity. People check the app on the go (phone, a few seconds between tasks) and at home (desktop, planning the week). Most visits are a glance: "what's due today, who's doing it, what's coming up."

## Product Purpose

Home management tool for coordinating shared household life: calendar events, chores, todos (kanban), and stats on who did what. Not a public SaaS; a private tool for trusted circles. Success means households stop juggling separate apps for this and actually use it together, every day.

## Brand Personality

Cozy, clean, readable, retro. Warm without being precious. Structured without being corporate. It should feel like a well-kept household almanac: dependable, printed-page clarity, a little seasonal warmth. Per-household theming is a first-class feature: different households should be able to make it feel like theirs.

## Anti-references

- Google's over-minimalism: clean is good, stripped-to-nothing is not
- Generic SaaS white-and-blue: this lives in someone's home, not an office
- Dense productivity tools (Jira, Asana): wrong register entirely
- Pastel "family app" look (Cozi-style): too soft, not enough structure

## Design Principles

1. **Glance first.** The home page answers "what's due today and who's on it" in one look. Every screen leads with the thing a household member came to check; detail comes second.
2. **Linear's bones, retro's skin.** Borrow Linear's structural clarity (clean rows, strong typographic rhythm, purposeful whitespace) and dress it in warm tones. Warmth over sterility; every surface should feel lived-in.
3. **Mobile is not a downgrade.** Calendar, chores, and the todo board work fully on a phone. A usable weekly view on mobile is a core gap to close.
4. **The household owns it.** Theming is identity, not decoration. Every design must survive a palette swap without breaking hierarchy, meaning, or contrast.
5. **Todos move, they don't pile up.** The kanban board should make progress visible and moving a card effortless, on touch and on keyboard.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA: 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for large text and UI boundaries, visible focus on every interactive element.
- Keyboard shortcuts required for core actions (navigate sections, add item, move kanban cards); built incrementally.
- Theming must never rely on a single hue for meaning; status always pairs color with a label, icon, or position.
- Any household palette must keep AA contrast; theme tooling should enforce it rather than trust it.
